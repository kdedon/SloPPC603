# Overall completion estimate

Current cross-system percentages, gaps and validation boundaries are tracked in
[SYSTEM_COMPLETION.md](../../SYSTEM_COMPLETION.md). Historical milestones below retain
their original scope and dates; later feature contracts supersede early limitations.

The [2026-09-23 full-scope audit](../../FULL_CPU_COMPLETION_AUDIT.md) supersedes the
current headline with **46.29% (about 46%)**; the round-40 table below is historical.


The estimate covers the full CPU-only 603e scope in [TASK_PLAN.md](TASK_PLAN.md), through P30: architectural features, dual dispatch, timing and 60x behavior, caches/MMU/floating point/modes, and verified FPGA delivery. Board-level work is excluded. It is a judgment-based estimate of completed scope, not an instruction-coverage percentage or a forecast of time remaining.

Use the same weights after each round. Credit accepted implementation and verification; give source preparation its own credit without also counting it as executable hardware. A small batch may leave the rounded overall percentage unchanged. Revise the weights only when scope changes, recording the reason.

It did not earn a 20–25% headline: the branch/LSU stream received 40% credit at that round for the bounded functional lane, with the remaining architecture and timing work explicit. LR/CTR is credited inside branches, not again as supervisor state. The generated program oracle does not earn full architectural-reference or timing-conformance credit. The original instruction-family queue resumes after this milestone.

Round 33 credit reflects executable capabilities in three previously incomplete areas. The 14 reviewed update forms earn source credit and their atomic architectural effects earn LSU credit. Bus credit is for the single-outstanding scalar master, adverse protocol checks and actual-core data integration; caches and coherency themselves remain unimplemented. The reference increment is for real handler execution and first-divergence checking, not whole-machine dispatch, full upstream vector acceptance, memory/FP comparisons or cycle conformance. The fixed weights are unchanged; the score is a scope estimate rather than a time-remaining forecast.

Reference credit reflects expanded independent state comparisons and accepted-edge timing evidence, not new instruction implementation or a full processor timing claim.
